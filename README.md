# My Car Parts

## 1. Project Overview

**My Car Parts** is a modern, responsive website for a parts and equipment agency serving customers looking for products and components for cars, generators, and heavy machinery. The website is intended to provide a clear, professional online presence where visitors can explore the business, browse relevant product and equipment categories, and find ways to contact the agency.

The visual identity combines an automotive and industrial feel. The brand uses a strong mechanical look that communicates reliability, equipment expertise, and a broad parts offering.

## 2. Brand Identity & Design Direction

All pages must follow the same visual identity established by the homepage.

### Brand presentation
- **Brand name:** My Car Parts
- **Tagline:** Cars, Generators & Heavy Machinery Parts
- **Business focus:** Automotive parts, generator parts and equipment, and heavy machinery parts
- **Design character:** Professional, modern, industrial, mechanical, and easy to navigate

### Core visual direction
Use the homepage as the single source of truth for:
- Brand logo and logo proportions
- Primary and secondary colors
- Typography, font weights, and heading styles
- Button shapes, colors, hover states, and icons
- Backgrounds, borders, shadows, and card styles
- Section spacing, content widths, and alignment
- Image presentation and aspect ratios
- Responsive behavior and mobile navigation

The logo's navy-blue, red, and industrial-yellow accents should be used consistently and in balance with the homepage palette. Do not introduce unrelated colors or a separate theme on inner pages.

## 3. Global Header & Navigation

The header must be consistent across the homepage and every internal page.

### Header requirements
- Display the official **My Car Parts** logo and brand identity.
- Keep the same header background, height, spacing, typography, and visual treatment as the homepage.
- Keep navigation links aligned and styled consistently.
- Use the same button and icon treatment throughout the website.
- Preserve the homepage's hover, active, and focus states.
- Ensure the header adapts properly to desktop, tablet, and mobile screens.

### Navigation behavior
- Navigation links must lead to their correct pages or sections.
- The active page should be visually identifiable using the homepage's existing active-link style.
- Mobile navigation must be easy to open, use, and close.
- Avoid page-specific header redesigns or duplicate navigation styles.

## 4. Homepage Design Standard

The homepage defines the overall design system for the website. Its layout, colors, visual hierarchy, and component styles should guide every other page.

Homepage sections should retain their existing content and functionality. When adding or updating sections, reuse the same:
- Section heading and subtitle styles
- Content container widths
- Card designs and image treatments
- Buttons and call-to-action styles
- Background and accent treatments
- Spacing and responsive grid behavior

Do not redesign the homepage when making inner pages consistent with it.

## 5. Website Pages & Content

Every page should feel like part of the same My Car Parts website.

Pages may have different content and layouts according to their purpose, but they must share the same design system.

### Product and category pages
Where applicable, product and category pages should:
- Present products or parts in a clear, organized layout.
- Use consistent product images, titles, descriptions, and information styles.
- Keep cards, category labels, and action buttons consistent with the homepage.
- Maintain readable spacing and responsive grids.
- Make product information easy to scan on mobile devices.

### Information and business pages
About, contact, service, or other information pages should:
- Use the same page title and section-heading styles.
- Follow the homepage's content width and spacing.
- Use the same buttons, icons, typography, and background treatments.
- Keep forms and information panels visually consistent with the brand.
- Provide clear navigation back to other website sections.

### General page consistency
- Do not create a separate theme for any inner page.
- Do not use default browser styling where the homepage has custom components.
- Do not mix unrelated templates or footer designs.
- Reuse common components wherever possible.
- Preserve the purpose and functionality of each individual page.

## 6. Global Footer — Mandatory on Every Page

**The complete homepage footer is the official footer design for the entire website.**

The homepage currently has a designed, fixed footer treatment, while some inner pages display a simplified footer. This inconsistency must be corrected.

### Required footer standard
Every page must display the same complete footer design as the homepage, including its existing:
- Footer background and color scheme
- Brand logo or brand-name presentation
- Typography and heading styles
- Link groups and navigation links
- Contact or business information, where present
- Social links or icons, where present
- Dividers, borders, spacing, and decorative details
- Copyright and designer/developer credit

Do not replace the homepage footer with a basic text-only footer on internal pages.

### Footer implementation
- Reuse one shared footer component across all pages.
- Keep its layout, content, colors, and styling consistent.
- Ensure every footer link works correctly.
- Make the footer responsive on mobile and tablet screens.
- If the homepage uses a fixed or sticky footer, preserve that intended behavior consistently across the site.
- Ensure page content has enough bottom spacing so the footer does not cover buttons, forms, product cards, or other content.
- Avoid overlapping or hidden content on short and long pages.

The footer should look and behave like the homepage footer everywhere.

## 7. Responsive & Mobile-First Requirements

The website must remain comfortable to use on desktop, tablet, and mobile devices.

### Responsive requirements
- Use fluid layouts and responsive containers.
- Stack multi-column sections appropriately on smaller screens.
- Keep navigation accessible on mobile.
- Prevent horizontal scrolling and content overflow.
- Scale images without distortion.
- Keep buttons and interactive elements comfortable to tap.
- Ensure text remains readable without excessive zooming.
- Check footer columns and links at narrow screen widths.
- Maintain consistent spacing and visual hierarchy across breakpoints.

## 8. UI Components & Interaction Standards

All shared interface components should use the homepage design language.

This includes:
- Navigation links and mobile menu
- Primary and secondary buttons
- Product and category cards
- Section headings and labels
- Forms and input fields
- Icons and badges
- Image containers
- Footer links and social icons
- Hover, focus, active, and disabled states

Interactive elements should provide clear feedback and should work as expected. Links and buttons must not be left as decorative elements if they appear clickable.

## 9. Accessibility & Usability

- Use readable color contrast.
- Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images.
- Use clear labels for form fields.
- Make navigation and controls keyboard accessible.
- Use visible focus states.
- Use descriptive link and button text.
- Keep heading levels in a logical order.
- Avoid relying on color alone to communicate important information.

The public repository indicates that this project uses a modern frontend structure based on React, TypeScript, and Vite.

**Repository:** https://github.com/orionfxservis/My-Car-Parts  
**Live deployment:** GitHub Pages

The project should retain its existing application structure and working functionality when the design is updated.

### Development and maintenance guidelines
- Keep shared UI elements reusable.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of header and footer markup.
- Keep styling organized and consistent.
- Preserve existing routes and navigation behavior.
- Test all pages after shared component or style changes.
- Verify the production build and deployed website after updates.
- Do not expose private keys, credentials, or environment secrets in public files.

## 11. SEO & Performance

- Give every page a relevant, unique title and meta description.
- Use semantic HTML and a logical heading structure.
- Add descriptive alt text to meaningful images.
- Use optimized image sizes and modern formats where appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessarily large assets and layout shifts.
- Ensure internal links work and pages are easy to navigate.
- Test page rendering on common desktop and mobile browsers.

## 12. Website Quality Checklist

Before publishing an update, verify the following:

- [ ] Homepage design and existing functionality are preserved.
- [ ] Every page uses the same brand colors and typography.
- [ ] Every page uses the same logo and header design.
- [ ] Navigation works on desktop and mobile.
- [ ] Inner pages follow the homepage's section and card styling.
- [ ] Every page uses the complete homepage footer.
- [ ] No inner page has a simplified or unrelated footer.
- [ ] Footer links and contact links work.
- [ ] Fixed or sticky footer behavior does not cover page content.
- [ ] All pages are responsive at mobile, tablet, and desktop sizes.
- [ ] Images load correctly and maintain their proportions.
- [ ] Forms, buttons, and interactive elements work.
- [ ] No horizontal overflow, broken links, or obvious layout issues remain.
- [ ] SEO titles, descriptions, and image alt text are present where appropriate.
